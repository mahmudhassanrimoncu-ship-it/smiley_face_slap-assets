## 1. The central question

> Did editing itself evolve so dramatically that the old standards of excellence were no longer enough?

**Answer this research supports:** *Partly.* The craft did expand, from mostly manipulating **time and motion** (speed ramps, shakes, flashes, beat sync) to **constructing images** (cut-outs, 3D camera, typography in depth, light, particles). The evidence that this expansion *alone* explains the shift in attention is weak. The platform changed in the same period (vertical feeds, paid Shorts, swipe-based metrics), the most successful newer channels sit in subjects with huge existing audiences (football in particular), and signature looks quickly became named "styles" that were copied down to phone apps. The documentary presents this as five **hypotheses** (§8), not as a proven conclusion.

## 6. How the new techniques differ from previous generations

**OBSERVED, generic:**
1. **From time to image.** Velocity editing reshapes *when* things happen; composition editing reshapes *what the frame is*.
2. **Depth.** Subjects are separated from backgrounds, so type, light and particles can sit *between* layers. A 3D camera then produces parallax.
3. **Pre-processing.** AI upscaling (Topaz) is named by four of the six channels. It plausibly serves as a clean-up stage before compositing (HYPOTHESIS: inferred from tool lists, not from project files).
4. **Planning cost.** A masked, depth-based transition needs pre-visualisation, tracking and roto. A ramp-flash-shake transition can be improvised on the timeline.
5. **Copyability.** Presets and "like" tutorials replicate surface looks quickly. Deep compositions are harder to replicate exactly (HYPOTHESIS).

## 8. Why the competitive landscape changed: five hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis | Supporting evidence | What would refute it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The stage changed.** Shorts became a paid, swipe-ranked feed, and newer editors built for it from the start. | [12]–[17]; analytics define viewed vs. swiped [1] | Older editors who moved to Shorts early growing at the same rate |
| 2 | **Subject gravity.** Football and blockbuster film bring huge existing audiences. | 3 of 6 channels are football [19][22][25]; football-edit discovery pages show very high engagement | Equally complex non-football channels growing as fast |
| 3 | **The name becomes the style.** Signature looks turn into brands that others teach by name. | Five "X-like" tutorials [36]–[40]; preset packs [43] | Channels with heavily copied styles not benefiting in attention |
| 4 | **Diffusion → arms race.** Phone recreations make looks common and push originators to escalate. | AE styles taught in Alight Motion / CapCut [37]–[40] | Styles staying rare despite tutorials |
| 5 | **Leverage.** Scripts, presets and AI tools multiply one editor's output. | [41]; Topaz named by 4 of 6 [19][22][25][27] | No measurable link between tool adoption and output or quality |

**Does editing complexity correlate with audience growth?** No published study was found. A proper test would need to: (a) sample many channels per niche; (b) score complexity per upload (layer count, roto, 3D camera, typography; for example, blind-rated by editors); (c) model views and subscriber deltas per upload while controlling for subject, upload timing, frequency, music trend and channel age. **Until then, it is a hypothesis.**

**Impact on established creators (HYPOTHESIS):** the velocity grammar became the community's shared language and therefore ordinary. Engagement drops can come from format changes, recommendation shifts, upload pauses, audience fatigue or quality, and outsiders cannot tell which without channel analytics. **No older creator is named or criticised in the film.**

## 10. The next generation (SPECULATION, as labelled on screen)

- Generated footage (e.g. Veo 3 in Shorts [18]) could make visual density cheap, as phone apps did for velocity edits.
- The scarce skill may move from effects to **taste**: moment selection, music choice, meaning.
- Restraint may become a differentiator ("what they choose to leave out").
