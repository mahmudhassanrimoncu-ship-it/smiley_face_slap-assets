**Final title:** **THE STANDARD MOVED**
*Subtitle:* How a new generation of editors captured the community's attention — and what really changed

**Alternative titles**
1. When Good Wasn't Enough: The New Generation of Editors
2. The Edit Arms Race

4. Did Editing Evolve, or Did the Stage Change?

**Thumbnail direction:** a split frame. Left half is a velocity-edit motion-blur smear (cool cyan). Right half is a layered composition with a subject cut-out in front of huge type (magenta and amber). **Runtime:** about 16 minutes (rendered at 1920×1080, 30 fps). Target range: 12–18 minutes.

---

## How to read each scene

- **Duration:** measured from the rendered file. It is independent of your voice-over speed, because each sentence card holds for a fixed reading time, not a spoken time.
- **Narration and subtitle:** the exact sentences shown at the bottom, in order. The same text is in `04-narration.md`.
- **Motion graphics:** written so you can rebuild or extend the scene in After Effects. Easing notation:
  - *expo-out* ≈ AE Easy Ease Out with 85–95% influence on the outgoing handle
  - *cubic in-out* ≈ Easy Ease with 60–70% influence on both handles
  - *quint in-out* ≈ 80%+ on both handles (whip-pan feel)
- **Global visual system:**
  - Near-black navy backdrop (#06080D) with a slow-drifting perspective floor grid, depth-parallax dust and a vignette.
  - The backdrop is driven by **global time**, so it never jumps at a cut. Every scene boundary is a seamless continuation.
  - Accent colours: cyan #3FE3FF, magenta #FF3D7F, amber #FFB648, green #4CF0A0, violet #8F7DFF.
  - Type: Inter 500–900, JetBrains Mono for data and labels.
- **Evidence pills:** green VERIFIED, cyan OBSERVED, amber HYPOTHESIS, magenta SPECULATION, grey NOTE. They sit above the subtitle bar, with bibliography numbers.
- **Footage slots:** hatched frames labelled `FOOTAGE SLOT xx` mark where real footage goes. See `07-footage-and-archival.md`.

## SCENE 6: Chapter 04, "The old grammar" · duration: see timing.json

**What viewers see:** a graph-editor panel (left) and a preview box (right).
1. **Time remap:** a speed curve draws (riser), labelled RAMP DOWN / SLOW-MO HOLD / SNAP. A playhead runs along it twice. The preview ball's position is the *integral* of the speed curve, so viewers see slow-mo and snap.
2. **Shake:** damped-sine curves for position (amber) and rotation (violet). The preview shakes with decaying amplitude.
3. **Zoom transition:** a scale-over-time curve peaks at "CUT HIDDEN HERE". Preview: shot A punches in on a cubic curve, a white flash hits, shot B lands with expo-out. It plays twice.
4. **Beat sync:** a waveform with magenta beat peaks. Dashed beat lines rise into the graph and keyframe diamonds pop on each beat.
5. **Many small decisions:** 46 keyframe diamonds scatter across the graph.
6. Summary table: TIME ✓, MOTION ✓, THE IMAGE ITSELF —. The last row turns magenta (foreshadowing).

**Information:** how the earlier grammar works, and the hypothesis that it mostly transforms time and motion.
**Transition out:** the table lifts and fades.

**Narration and subtitles**
37. So let's look at how that grammar actually works.
38. A velocity edit is a time remap: the clip's speed changes inside the shot, not between shots.
39. On a speed graph, the curve drops into slow motion, holds, then accelerates sharply into the next beat.
40. The shake is a separate layer of motion: fast, decaying offsets in position and rotation, often with motion blur.
41. A flash or zoom transition hides the cut at its fastest frame, so the eye reads one continuous movement.
42. All of it is locked to the audio. The beat decides where the energy lands.
43. Done well, this is real craft. A ramp that feels right is the result of many small decisions.
44. But notice what it mostly transforms: time and motion, rather than the image itself. — *HYPOTHESIS*
45. That distinction matters for what happened next.

## SCENE 9: Chapter 07, "Anatomy of a modern edit" · duration: see timing.json

**What viewers see (the showcase):** an **exploded isometric layer stack**. Each plane is rotated −28° and scaled 0.55 on Y, spaced 92 px apart. Each layer has a flat label on the right.
1. "Built for this film · not from any creator" tag.
2. FOOTAGE plate (graded gradient, stadium lights, optional slot A01).
3. SUBJECT silhouette, with a dashed mask path tracing around it (rotoscope).
4. "LEGEND" type layer *between* footage and subject.
5. A 3D-camera icon moves. Each layer shifts horizontally in proportion to its depth (0.15 → 1.0), which shows parallax.
6. LIGHT (radial glow plus anamorphic streak).
7. PARTICLES.
8. Motion-blur ghost trail on the subject, plus a velocity mini-graph.
9. **Collapse:** spacing → 0, rotation → 0, Y-scale → 1 (cubic in-out 1.4 s, heavy whoosh). The stack becomes one composed frame, then a slow 1.18× push with parallax drift.
10. The frame slides left. "BUILDING IMAGES, NOT ONLY CUTTING THEM" appears with skill chips: compositing, masking/roto, 3D, typography, timing.

**AE rebuild notes:** use a 3D-layer pre-comp per plane at Z = −400…+400, and a two-node camera with Point of Interest. Animate Z-spacing with a slider (e.g. `z = index * thisComp.layer("CTRL").effect("Spread")("Slider")`) to collapse the stack.
**Transition out:** the composition and chips scale to 0.7 and fade.

**Narration and subtitles**
68. What does a modern, high-density edit actually contain?
69. Here is a generic example, built for this film. It is not taken from any creator. — *NOTE*
70. At the bottom: the source footage, upscaled and colour graded.
71. Above it, the subject, cut out from the background with a mask or rotoscope, so it can be separated in depth.
72. Behind the subject, typography. It is huge, kinetic and partly hidden, so the subject appears to stand in front of the words.
73. A 3D camera moves through these layers. Because each one sits at a different depth, they move at different speeds. That is parallax.
74. Then light: glows, flares and light leaks that make separate layers feel like one image.
75. Particles, grain and texture fill the empty space.
76. Finally, motion blur and velocity ramps tie the whole composition to the rhythm of the track.
77. Collapse the stack, and it reads as a single image moving through space.
78. This is the real shift: the edit is no longer only cutting footage. It is building new images out of it. — *HYPOTHESIS*
79. That takes compositing, masking, 3D, typography and timing: skills from very different disciplines.
